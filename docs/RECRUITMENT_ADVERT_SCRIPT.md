# "Bring Your Campus": Campus Rep Recruitment Advert

A director's script for a short video asking students to apply as Adbox campus
representatives. The lead role can be played by any actor, male or female. The
lines, actions, and camera directions stay the same.

The facts here come from the Rep Recruitment Pack and the Campus Representatives
Launch Playbook (29 September 2026). If the brief changes, update this file first.

---

## 1. At a glance

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Working title | **Bring Your Campus** |
| Main cut | 60 seconds, 6 scenes |
| Cutdowns | 30 seconds and 15 seconds (section 8) |
| Format | Vertical 9:16, 1080×1920, for TikTok, Instagram Reels, WhatsApp Status, and X. Also film a 16:9 safety wide of every scene |
| Tone | Warm, real, and a little funny. A student talking to students, not a corporate advert |
| Language | Plain English. Light Ghanaian expressions are fine where marked as optional |
| Shoot time | One day: morning, afternoon, and evening light (section 4) |
| Message in one line | *Adbox is a new Ghanaian app for campus life, and we need two students at [CAMPUS] to help launch it.* |

### Fill these blanks before the shoot

The advert says these words out loud, so they must be **final, approved by the
launch lead, and identical on every campus**. Never improvise them on set.

| Placeholder | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `[CAMPUS]` | The institution being filmed or targeted |
| `[HOURS]` | Hours per week (the playbook suggests about 4–6) |
| `[TERM]` | How long the role runs, for example a 6- or 8-week launch term |
| `[WHAT REPS GET]` | The approved benefit. **If nothing is approved, leave it out of the advert entirely** |
| `[DEADLINE]` | A real closing date |
| `[APPLY LINK]` | The application form link, plus its QR code |
| `[CONTACT]` | One name and one channel for questions |

## 2. The rules this advert must never break

These come straight from the recruitment pack and playbook. Stop the take if
any of them is broken.

1. **No promise of pay for using the app.** Students do not get paid to use
   Adbox. In the advert, the lead says this honestly on camera (Scene 3).
2. **No invented benefits.** Mention only the approved `[WHAT REPS GET]` wording,
   and only on the end card or in the final line.
3. **Students sign themselves up.** The lead never takes a student's phone to
   create an account for them. The lead can point at the screen, but the student
   does the tapping.
4. **Describe the app accurately:** community posts, campus stories, and surveys
   students run themselves. Do not show or mention features that aren't in the
   release.
5. **No pressure.** Students are approached politely, and declining is OK.
   Show at least one person saying "maybe later" and the lead taking it well.
6. **Real campus, real students.** No fake testimonials, and nobody plays an
   "Adbox staff member".
7. **No real personal data on screen.** Phones show a test account with
   made-up names. No real numbers, emails, or group chats.

## 3. Characters

All names are gender-neutral so either a male or female actor can play any
role. Use the actor's real first name if they prefer, but keep it consistent.

| Character | Who they are | Casting notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SENA** (lead) | A current student who is friendly, organised, and the person who gets the group chat moving | Any gender. Must feel like a real student, not a presenter. Comfortable speaking to camera. Good energy without shouting |
| **ELI** | Sena's friend, walking with them after a lecture | Any gender. Light comic presence |
| **STUDENT A, "the sceptic"** | Sitting at the food court and suspicious of new apps | Any gender. Dry, natural delivery. Asks the pay question |
| **STUDENT B, "the curious one"** | At the same table and quietly interested | Any gender. Warm reactions; doesn't need many lines |
| **STUDENT C, "maybe later"** | Busy, politely declines | Any gender. One line |
| **SESSION GROUP** | 5–8 students in a hall common room | Mixed genders, programmes, and phones (Android and iPhone) |
| **VOICEOVER** (optional) | Used only in the 15-second cut | Can be Sena's voice |

### How to direct the lead, whether male or female

- **The lines are identical.** Do not rewrite jokes or lines for male or female actors.
- Do not direct the lead to flirt, use their looks to win people over, or be
  "cute". The lead is confident because they are helpful, not because of charm.
- **Wardrobe:** everyday campus clothes that the actor would really wear,
  such as a shirt, top, or polo with jeans, trousers, or a skirt. Choose solid colours over
  big logos (no other brands on camera). Optional: an Adbox-coloured
  (purple/blue) accent. The lead wears the **same outfit in every scene**, because the
  story happens in one day.
- **Body language:** open posture, phone held so others can see the screen,
  stands at the side of a table rather than looming over it, keeps
  respectful distance with strangers of any gender, and makes eye contact and smiles.

## 4. Locations

Put these where students naturally stop, not where they walk fast. Get written
permission from the institution or venue manager before filming, and from anyone who is
recognisable on camera (section 10).

| # | Location | Time of day | What we need there |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | **Hostel or hall room** | Morning, window light | A tidy, real student room. A desk with notebooks and a phone charger. No visible brand posters |
| L2 | **Corridor outside a lecture hall** | Late morning, as a lecture lets out | Background students moving naturally. Notice board visible if possible |
| L3 | **Food court / canteen** | Lunchtime (12:00–14:00) | One table with two seated students, food on the table, light background noise |
| L4 | **Hall common room / JCR** | Afternoon | Chairs in a loose circle, 5–8 students, plug points for phones |
| L5 | **Library steps or a quiet bench** | Golden hour / early evening | Calm, warm light. Sena alone with a phone |
| L6 | **Campus notice board or main gate** | Evening, before dark | A printed Adbox rep poster with a working QR code pinned up |

**Backup if a location falls through:** L3 can be any outdoor seating area. L4
can be a classroom after hours. L6 can be any wall with the poster.

---

## 5. The script: 60-second main cut

**How to read each scene:** *Action* is what we see. **Dialogue** is what is said,
word for word. *Performance* is how to play it. **Director's check** is how to
judge whether the take is right or wrong.

---

### SCENE 1: The hook

**Location:** L1, hostel room · **Time:** morning · **Length:** 0:00–0:06

**Shots**
1. Close-up: a phone buzzing on the desk with a group-chat notification ("Hall 3 Football 🔥 · 48 messages").
2. Medium shot: SENA, sitting on the bed, picks up the phone, looks up, and talks straight to camera.

*Action:* Sena glances at the phone, laughs at a message, then turns to the lens.

**SENA** *(to camera, easy smile)*
> If you're the person who always gets the group chat moving…
> *(beat, holds up phone)*
> …Adbox wants to meet you.

*Performance:* As if talking to a friend across the room. Relaxed and
conversational, not like a "presenter voice". A small eyebrow raise on "you".

**Director's check**
- ✅ **Right:** We believe this is Sena's real room and real morning. The hook
  line is clear in the first 3 seconds. The phone screen is a mock group chat.
- ❌ **Wrong:** Shouting "HEY GUYS!", over-the-top YouTuber energy, a real
  chat or real names visible on screen, or the line landing after 4+ seconds
  (people scroll past).

---

### SCENE 2: What Adbox is

**Location:** L2, lecture hall corridor · **Time:** late morning · **Length:** 0:06–0:15

**Shots**
1. Tracking shot (walk-and-talk): SENA and ELI leaving the lecture hall with bags on shoulders.
2. Insert: Sena's phone showing the Adbox feed with a campus post and a survey card.

*Action:* Eli complains about something small. Sena shows Eli the phone while walking.

**ELI** *(stretching)*
> That lecture was long, eh.

**SENA** *(pulling out phone)*
> Look at this. Adbox. It's a new Ghanaian app for campus life:
> posts from our campus, and surveys students run themselves.

**ELI** *(leans in, interested)*
> Wait, *we* make the surveys?

**SENA**
> We make the surveys.

*Performance:* Natural pace, like two friends who walk together every day.
Eli's "Wait, *we* make the surveys?" is the moment we sell, so play it as
genuinely surprised, not sarcastic.

**Director's check**
- ✅ **Right:** The two words *posts* and *surveys* are heard clearly. The screen
  insert shows the **Surveys** feature (the part nothing else on a student's
  phone does). Background students behave normally and nobody looks at the lens.
- ❌ **Wrong:** Sena lists every feature ("Shorts, Glances, Discover…"),
  which is too much. The app screen shows anything not in the current release.
  Extras waving at the camera.

---

### SCENE 3: The honest question (the heart of the advert)

**Location:** L3, food court · **Time:** lunchtime · **Length:** 0:15–0:32

**Shots**
1. Wide: Sena approaches a table where STUDENT A and STUDENT B are eating.
2. Over-the-shoulder on Student A for the question.
3. Medium close-up on Sena for the answer. **This is the most important shot in the film.**
4. Close-up: Student B scanning the QR code **on their own phone**.
5. Cutaway: STUDENT C at the next table, waving a polite "not now".

*Action:* Sena stops at the side of the table and doesn't sit until invited.

**SENA** *(friendly, not pushy)*
> Hi, sorry to disturb your food. I'm Sena, I'm helping launch Adbox here.
> Two minutes?

**STUDENT A** *(suspicious, chewing)*
> Hmm. Is this one of those apps…
> *(points with fork)*
> …will I get paid for using it?

**SENA** *(honest, smiling, no hesitation)*
> No. Honestly, it's not that kind of app.
> It's where [CAMPUS] posts, asks questions, and argues about
> *(turns phone to them)*
> …which hall has the best jollof.

**STUDENT B** *(laughs, already reaching for own phone)*
> Ah, but that one, we know the answer.

**STUDENT A** *(rolls eyes, but smiles)*
> Send me the link.

*(Cut to STUDENT C at the next table, holding up a hand.)*

**STUDENT C**
> Maybe later. I have a quiz.

**SENA** *(thumbs up, genuinely fine with it)*
> No wahala. Good luck!

*(Optional local flavour: "No wahala" can be "No problem" if the actor prefers.)*

*Performance:* Sena's "No. Honestly" must come **immediately**, with no awkward
pause or guilty look. The honesty is what makes Sena likeable. Student A's
suspicion should be funny and relatable, not rude.

**Director's check**
- ✅ **Right:** Sena clearly says **"No"** to the pay question. Student B
  installs or scans **on their own phone, with their own hands**. Student C's
  "maybe later" is accepted happily. Sena stands at the table's side until
  welcomed. The jollof survey on screen is a test survey.
- ❌ **Wrong:**
  - Sena dodges the pay question, says "kind of", or hints at money. **Stop
    and reshoot.** This breaks rule 1.
  - Sena takes Student B's phone to sign them up (breaks rule 3).
  - Sena keeps pushing Student C after the "no" (breaks rule 5).
  - Sena leans over or touches the students. The scene should feel safe and casual.

---

### SCENE 4: The session

**Location:** L4, hall common room · **Time:** afternoon · **Length:** 0:32–0:44

**Shots**
1. Wide: 5–8 students in a loose circle, with Sena standing at the front holding the phone up.
2. Close-ups: a mix of Android and iPhone screens, with each student signing up on their own phone.
3. Medium shot: one student looking confused. Sena kneels beside them to see the screen.
4. Insert: Sena writing in a notebook: *"OTP slow, step 3."*

**SENA** *(to the group, warm)*
> Okay, everyone's on the feed? Now post one thing about your hall.

**STUDENT (any)** *(frustrated, holding phone up)*
> Mine is stuck. The code didn't come.

**SENA** *(kneels beside them, writes it down, unbothered)*
> Perfect. That's exactly what the team needs to know.

*Performance:* The bug is a **good** moment, not an embarrassing one. Sena is
pleased to catch it. Keep the session lively: people show each other screens and
someone laughs at a post.

**Director's check**
- ✅ **Right:** The group mixes genders, phone types, and personalities. Everyone
  taps their own phone. Sena reacts to the problem calmly and positively,
  and the notebook insert is legible.
- ❌ **Wrong:** Sena hides the problem or says "it always works". Sena signs
  up for someone. Everyone looks bored or scripted. Only one phone type
  appears on screen.

---

### SCENE 5: What the job really is

**Location:** L5, library steps · **Time:** golden hour · **Length:** 0:44–0:52

**Shots**
1. Medium shot: Sena sitting on the steps, typing a short report into a phone (mock WhatsApp "Adbox Reps" group).
2. Sena looks up, straight to camera.

**SENA** *(to camera, calm and sincere)*
> That's the job.
> Sign people up. Show them how it works.
> Tell the team what breaks.

*Performance:* Slower and quieter than the earlier scenes. This is the honest
"here's what you're signing up for" beat. Give each of the three phrases its own breath.

**Director's check**
- ✅ **Right:** The three tasks are heard clearly and in this order. The report
  on screen is a short, believable message, for example "12 signups today, OTP slow on step 3."
- ❌ **Wrong:** Rushed delivery, or making the job sound bigger or easier than
  it is ("you'll be famous", "it's no work at all"). Real group names or numbers on screen.

---

### SCENE 6: The call to apply

**Location:** L6, notice board or campus gate · **Time:** evening · **Length:** 0:52–1:00

**Shots**
1. Medium shot: Sena pins (or points to) the Adbox rep poster with the QR code.
2. Sena turns to camera for the final line.
3. End card (added in edit, 3 seconds).

**SENA** *(to camera, confident, inviting)*
> We need two reps on [CAMPUS].
> [HOURS] a week, for [TERM].
> Apply by [DEADLINE]. The link is right here.
> *(taps poster)*

**END CARD** *(on screen, no voice needed)*
> **BE AN ADBOX CAMPUS REP**
> [CAMPUS] · 2 positions
> Sign students up. Show them how it works. Tell us what breaks.
> [HOURS] a week · [TERM] · [WHAT REPS GET] ← *only if approved*
> **Apply by [DEADLINE]** · [APPLY LINK] · QR code
> Questions: [CONTACT]

*Performance:* A clear, friendly invitation. Not begging and not a hard sell. A
small nod and smile to finish.

**Director's check**
- ✅ **Right:** Every placeholder is read exactly as written in the approved
  brief. The QR code on the poster is in focus and **actually scans** (test it
  from the monitor). The end card stays up for at least 3 seconds.
- ❌ **Wrong:** The actor invents a benefit ("you'll get data and money!").
  Wrong deadline or link. A blurry QR. The end card is too short to read.

energy build naturally.

## 7. Sound, music, and captions

- Record clean dialogue with a lavalier or shotgun mic. At the food court, also
  record 30 seconds of room tone.
- Use **royalty-free or properly licensed music** only. A light Afrobeats or highlife
  instrumental fits. Duck the music under every line of dialogue.
- **Burn in captions** on every cut, because most people watch on mute. Use the AdBox fonts
  and brand colours from the approved brand assets.
- Keep Ghanaian expressions in the captions as spoken ("No wahala").

---

## 8. Cutdowns

**30-second cut:** Scene 1 (hook) → Scene 3 (pay question, ending at "Send me
the link") → Scene 5 (the three tasks) → Scene 6 (call to apply) → end card.

**15-second cut:** Scene 3 pay question and answer only → voiceover over the poster:
*"Be an Adbox campus rep at [CAMPUS]. Apply by [DEADLINE]."* → end card.

The pay-question moment is in **every** cut on purpose. It is the most honest
and most shareable part of the advert.

## 9. Director's on-set checklist

### Before rolling
- [ ] All placeholders filled in and approved by the launch lead
- [ ] Location permissions in hand for L1–L6
- [ ] Signed release forms from every recognisable person (section 10)
- [ ] All phones logged into the **test account** with mock names, chats, and survey
- [ ] Notifications silenced on every phone, and no real messages visible
- [ ] Poster QR code tested and opening the correct `[APPLY LINK]`
- [ ] No other brands visible (clothes, posters, laptop stickers)
- [ ] Lead's outfit photographed for continuity

### Judging every take
Ask these five questions. A take that fails any of them is not usable.

1. **Is it true?** Nothing said or shown promises pay, invents a benefit, or
   shows a feature not in the release.
2. **Is it clear?** Can you hear the key line once, without the music?
3. **Is it respectful?** Students tap their own phones, a "no" is accepted, and personal
   space is kept.
4. **Is it real?** Does it look like students on a real campus day, not an advert?
5. **Is it consistent?** Same outfit, same props, same phone, and the light matches the time of day.

### Common mistakes and the fix

| You see… | Why it's wrong | Say to the actor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hesitation on "will I get paid?" | Looks like hiding something | "Answer instantly. You're proud there's no catch." |
| Lead grabs a student's phone | Breaks the "students sign themselves up" rule | "Point, don't touch. Let them tap." |
| Presenter voice | Feels like an advert, so people scroll past | "Say it like you're telling your roommate." |
| Too many features listed | Confusing, and may promise things not live | "Only two words: posts and surveys." |
| Pushing after a "maybe later" | Feels like pressure | "Let them go happily. That's the point of the scene." |
| Extras staring at the lens | Breaks the realism | "Carry on with what you were doing. We're not here." |
| Rushed final call to action | Viewers miss the deadline | "One breath per line. Land the date." |

## 10. Consent, privacy, and permissions

- Every recognisable person signs a video release form, including extras
  and the session group. Under-18s need a parent or guardian signature.
- Get written permission from hall management or the institution for each location.
- Use no real applicant data, real student contacts, or real group chats.
  All phone screens show a test account.
- Do not film people who haven't agreed. Blur them in the edit or reshoot.
- Keep raw footage on approved team storage only.
