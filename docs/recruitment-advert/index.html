Bring Your Campus

A short recruitment advert asking students to apply as Adbox campus representatives. Any actor, male or female, can play the lead. Their lines, actions and camera directions stay the same.

  • 60s main cut
  • 30s & 15s cutdowns
  • Vertical 9:16
  • 6 scenes · 6 locations
  • Any-gender lead

02

The rules this advert must never break

These come straight from the Rep Recruitment Pack and Launch Playbook. Stop the take if any of them is broken.

  1. No promise of pay for using the app. Students do not get paid to use Adbox. In the advert, the lead says this honestly on camera (Scene 3).
  2. No invented benefits. Mention only the approved [WHAT REPS GET] wording, and only on the end card or in the final line.
  3. Students sign themselves up. The lead never takes a student's phone to create an account for them. The lead can point at the screen, but the student does the tapping.
  4. Describe the app accurately: community posts, campus stories, and surveys students run themselves. Do not show or mention features that aren't in the release.
  5. No pressure. Students are approached politely, and declining is OK. Show at least one person saying "maybe later" and the lead taking it well.
  6. Real campus, real students. No fake testimonials, and nobody plays an "Adbox staff member".
  7. No real personal data on screen. Phones show a test account with made-up names. No real numbers, emails or group chats.
03

Characters

All names are gender-neutral so either a male or female actor can play any role. Use the actor's real first name if they prefer, but keep it consistent.

CharacterWho they areCasting notes
SENA (lead)A current student who is friendly, organised, and the person who gets the group chat movingAny gender. Must feel like a real student, not a presenter. Comfortable speaking to camera. Good energy without shouting.
ELISena's friend, walking with them after a lectureAny gender. Light comic presence.
STUDENT A, "the sceptic"Sitting at the food court and suspicious of new appsAny gender. Dry, natural delivery. Asks the pay question.
STUDENT B, "the curious one"At the same table and quietly interestedAny gender. Warm reactions; doesn't need many lines.
STUDENT C, "maybe later"Busy, politely declinesAny gender. One line.
SESSION GROUP5–8 students in a hall common roomMixed genders, programmes and phones (Android and iPhone).
VOICEOVER (optional)Used only in the 15-second cutCan be Sena's voice.

How to direct the lead, whether male or female

  • The lines are identical. Do not rewrite jokes or lines for male or female actors.
  • Do not direct the lead to flirt, use their looks to win people over, or be "cute". The lead is confident because they are helpful, not because of charm.
  • Wardrobe: everyday campus clothes that the actor would really wear, such as a shirt, top or polo with jeans, trousers or a skirt. Choose solid colours over big logos (no other brands on camera). Optional: an Adbox-coloured (purple/blue) accent. The lead wears the same outfit in every scene, because the story happens in one day.
  • Body language: open posture, phone held so others can see the screen, stands at the side of a table rather than looming over it, keeps respectful distance with strangers of any gender, and makes eye contact and smiles.

05

The script: 60-second main cut

Action is what we see. Dialogue is what is said, word for word. Performance is how to play it. Director's check is how to judge whether the take is right or wrong.

Scene 1The hook

L1 · Hostel roomMorning0:00–0:06
Shots
  1. Close-up: a phone buzzing on the desk with a group-chat notification ("Hall 3 Football 🔥 · 48 messages").
  2. Medium shot: SENA, sitting on the bed, picks up the phone, looks up, and talks straight to camera.
Action

Sena glances at the phone, laughs at a message, then turns to the lens.

Dialogue
Sena(to camera, easy smile)

If you're the person who always gets the group chat moving…

(beat, holds up phone)

…Adbox wants to meet you.

Performance

As if talking to a friend across the room. Relaxed and conversational, not like a "presenter voice". A small eyebrow raise on "you".

Director's check

✅ Right

  • We believe this is Sena's real room and real morning.
  • The hook line is clear in the first 3 seconds.
  • The phone screen is a mock group chat.

❌ Wrong

  • Shouting "HEY GUYS!" or over-the-top YouTuber energy.
  • A real chat or real names visible on screen.
  • The line lands after 4+ seconds (people scroll past).

Scene 2What Adbox is

L2 · Lecture corridorLate morning0:06–0:15
Shots
  1. Tracking shot (walk-and-talk): SENA and ELI leaving the lecture hall with bags on shoulders.
  2. Insert: Sena's phone showing the Adbox feed with a campus post and a survey card.
Action

Eli complains about something small. Sena shows Eli the phone while walking.

Dialogue
Eli(stretching)

That lecture was long, eh.

Sena(pulling out phone)

Look at this. Adbox. It's a new Ghanaian app for campus life: posts from our campus, and surveys students run themselves.

Eli(leans in, interested)

Wait, we make the surveys?

Sena

We make the surveys.

Performance

Natural pace, like two friends who walk together every day. Eli's "Wait, we make the surveys?" is the moment we sell, so play it as genuinely surprised, not sarcastic.

Director's check

✅ Right

  • The two words posts and surveys are heard clearly.
  • The screen insert shows the Surveys feature (the part nothing else on a student's phone does).
  • Background students behave normally and nobody looks at the lens.

❌ Wrong

  • Sena lists every feature ("Shorts, Glances, Discover…"), which is too much.
  • The app screen shows anything not in the current release.
  • Extras waving at the camera.

Scene 3The honest question (the heart of the advert)

L3 · Food courtLunchtime0:15–0:32
Shots
  1. Wide: Sena approaches a table where STUDENT A and STUDENT B are eating.
  2. Over-the-shoulder on Student A for the question.
  3. Medium close-up on Sena for the answer. This is the most important shot in the film.
  4. Close-up: Student B scanning the QR code on their own phone.
  5. Cutaway: STUDENT C at the next table, waving a polite "not now".
Action

Sena stops at the side of the table and doesn't sit until invited.

Dialogue
Sena(friendly, not pushy)

Hi, sorry to disturb your food. I'm Sena, I'm helping launch Adbox here. Two minutes?

Student A(suspicious, chewing)

Hmm. Is this one of those apps…

(points with fork)

…will I get paid for using it?

Sena(honest, smiling, no hesitation)

No. Honestly, it's not that kind of app. It's where [CAMPUS] posts, asks questions, and argues about

(turns phone to them)

…which hall has the best jollof.

Student B(laughs, already reaching for own phone)

Ah, but that one, we know the answer.

Student A(rolls eyes, but smiles)

Send me the link.

(Cut to STUDENT C at the next table, holding up a hand.)

Student C

Maybe later. I have a quiz.

Sena(thumbs up, genuinely fine with it)

No wahala. Good luck!

Optional local flavour: "No wahala" can be "No problem" if the actor prefers.

Performance

Sena's "No. Honestly" must come immediately, with no awkward pause or guilty look. The honesty is what makes Sena likeable. Student A's suspicion should be funny and relatable, not rude.

Director's check

✅ Right

  • Sena clearly says "No" to the pay question.
  • Student B installs or scans on their own phone, with their own hands.
  • Student C's "maybe later" is accepted happily.
  • Sena stands at the table's side until welcomed.
  • The jollof survey on screen is a test survey.

❌ Wrong

  • STOP & RESHOOT Sena dodges the pay question, says "kind of", or hints at money. This breaks rule 1.
  • Sena takes Student B's phone to sign them up (breaks rule 3).
  • Sena keeps pushing Student C after the "no" (breaks rule 5).
  • Sena leans over or touches the students. The scene should feel safe and casual.

Scene 4The session

L4 · Common roomAfternoon0:32–0:44
Shots
  1. Wide: 5–8 students in a loose circle, with Sena standing at the front holding the phone up.
  2. Close-ups: a mix of Android and iPhone screens, with each student signing up on their own phone.
  3. Medium shot: one student looking confused. Sena kneels beside them to see the screen.
  4. Insert: Sena writing in a notebook: "OTP slow, step 3."
Dialogue
Sena(to the group, warm)

Okay, everyone's on the feed? Now post one thing about your hall.

Student (any)(frustrated, holding phone up)

Mine is stuck. The code didn't come.

Sena(kneels beside them, writes it down, unbothered)

Perfect. That's exactly what the team needs to know.

Performance

The bug is a good moment, not an embarrassing one. Sena is pleased to catch it. Keep the session lively: people show each other screens and someone laughs at a post.

Director's check

✅ Right

  • The group mixes genders, phone types and personalities.
  • Everyone taps their own phone.
  • Sena reacts to the problem calmly and positively.
  • The notebook insert is legible.

❌ Wrong

  • Sena hides the problem or says "it always works".
  • Sena signs up for someone.
  • Everyone looks bored or scripted.
  • Only one phone type appears on screen.

Scene 5What the job really is

L5 · Library stepsGolden hour0:44–0:52
Shots
  1. Medium shot: Sena sitting on the steps, typing a short report into a phone (mock WhatsApp "Adbox Reps" group).
  2. Sena looks up, straight to camera.
Dialogue
Sena(to camera, calm and sincere)

That's the job.

Sign people up. Show them how it works.

Tell the team what breaks.

Performance

Slower and quieter than the earlier scenes. This is the honest "here's what you're signing up for" beat. Give each of the three phrases its own breath.

Director's check

✅ Right

  • The three tasks are heard clearly and in this order.
  • The report on screen is a short, believable message, for example "12 signups today, OTP slow on step 3."

❌ Wrong

  • Rushed delivery.
  • Making the job sound bigger or easier than it is ("you'll be famous", "it's no work at all").
  • Real group names or numbers on screen.

Scene 6The call to apply

L6 · Notice boardEvening0:52–1:00
Shots
  1. Medium shot: Sena pins (or points to) the Adbox rep poster with the QR code.
  2. Sena turns to camera for the final line.
  3. End card (added in edit, 3 seconds).
Dialogue
Sena(to camera, confident, inviting)

We need two reps on [CAMPUS].

[HOURS] a week, for [TERM].

Apply by [DEADLINE]. The link is right here.

(taps poster)
End card (on screen, no voice needed)
[CAMPUS] · 2 positions BE AN ADBOX CAMPUS REP Sign students up. Show them how it works. Tell us what breaks. [HOURS] a week · [TERM] · [WHAT REPS GET]*
QR CODE
Apply by [DEADLINE] [APPLY LINK] · Questions: [CONTACT]

* Show [WHAT REPS GET] only if the launch lead has approved it.

Performance

A clear, friendly invitation. Not begging and not a hard sell. A small nod and smile to finish.

Director's check

✅ Right

  • Every placeholder is read exactly as written in the approved brief.
  • The QR code on the poster is in focus and actually scans (test it from the monitor).
  • The end card stays up for at least 3 seconds.

❌ Wrong

  • The actor invents a benefit ("you'll get data and money!").
  • Wrong deadline or link.
  • A blurry QR.
  • The end card is too short to read.

07

Sound, music and captions

  • Record clean dialogue with a lavalier or shotgun mic. At the food court, also record 30 seconds of room tone.
  • Use royalty-free or properly licensed music only. A light Afrobeats or highlife instrumental fits. Duck the music under every line of dialogue.
  • Burn in captions on every cut, because most people watch on mute. Use the AdBox fonts and brand colours from the approved brand assets.
  • Keep Ghanaian expressions in the captions as spoken ("No wahala").
08

Cutdowns

30-second cut

Scene 1 (hook) → Scene 3 (pay question, ending at "Send me the link") → Scene 5 (the three tasks) → Scene 6 (call to apply) → end card.

15-second cut

Scene 3 pay question and answer only → voiceover over the poster: "Be an Adbox campus rep at [CAMPUS]. Apply by [DEADLINE]." → end card.

The pay-question moment is in every cut on purpose. It is the most honest and most shareable part of the advert.

Judging every take

Ask these five questions. A take that fails any of them is not usable.

  1. Is it true? Nothing said or shown promises pay, invents a benefit, or shows a feature not in the release.
  2. Is it clear? Can you hear the key line once, without the music?
  3. Is it respectful? Students tap their own phones, a "no" is accepted, and personal space is kept.
  4. Is it real? Does it look like students on a real campus day, not an advert?
  5. Is it consistent? Same outfit, same props, same phone, and the light matches the time of day.

10

Consent, privacy and permissions

  • Every recognisable person signs a video release form, including extras and the session group. Under-18s need a parent or guardian signature.
  • Get written permission from hall management or the institution for each location.
  • Use no real applicant data, real student contacts or real group chats. All phone screens show a test account.
  • Do not film people who haven't agreed. Blur them in the edit or reshoot.
  • Keep raw footage on approved team storage only.
